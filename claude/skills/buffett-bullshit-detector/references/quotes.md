These are well-attested quotes from letters, annual meetings, speeches, *Poor Charlie's Almanack*, and on-the-record interviews. Where a quote is widely paraphrased, the most common form is given. Source noted where useful.

## 1. On Valuation & Price

> "Price is what you pay; value is what you get."  — Buffett

> "Whether we're talking about socks or stocks, I like buying quality merchandise when it is marked down."  — Buffett

> "If you knew the price was going to fall in half tomorrow, would you double up? If you wouldn't, then maybe you don't really like this stock."  — Munger (paraphrased; the spirit appears repeatedly)

> "The investor of today does not profit from yesterday's growth."  — Buffett

> "A great business at a fair price is superior to a fair business at a great price."  — Buffett (often credited to Munger, since Charlie famously taught Warren this)

## 2. On Forecasting & Predictions

> "Forecasts may tell you a great deal about the forecaster; they tell you nothing about the future."  — Buffett

> "The only value of stock forecasters is to make fortune-tellers look good."  — Buffett

> "If past history was all that was needed to play the game of money, the richest people would be librarians."  — Buffett

> "Charlie and I never have an opinion on the market because it wouldn't be any good and it might interfere with the opinions we have that are good."  — Buffett

## 3. On Management & People

> "We look for three things when we hire people. We look for intelligence, we look for initiative or energy, and we look for integrity. And if they don't have the latter, the first two will kill you, because if you're going to get someone without integrity, you want them lazy and dumb."  — Buffett

> "It takes 20 years to build a reputation and five minutes to ruin it. If you think about that, you'll do things differently."  — Buffett

> "Honesty is a very expensive gift, don't expect it from cheap people."  — Buffett

> "You should marry the person who has the same values as you do. If you do that, you've got the most important decision in life nine-tenths made correctly."  — Buffett (on people generally — he applies it to business partnerships too)

> "It's better to hang out with people better than you. Pick out associates whose behavior is better than yours and you'll drift in that direction."  — Buffett

## 4. On Reputation & Character

> "Lose money for the firm and I will be understanding. Lose a shred of reputation for the firm and I will be ruthless."  — Buffett

> "Should you find yourself in a chronically leaking boat, energy devoted to changing vessels is likely to be more productive than energy devoted to patching leaks."  — Buffett (on persistently bad management or persistently bad situations)

> "There seems to be some perverse human characteristic that likes to make easy things difficult."  — Buffett

> "In looking for people to hire, look for three qualities: integrity, intelligence, and energy. And if they don't have the first, the other two will kill you."  — Buffett (the canonical short form)

## 5. On Incentives

> "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome."  — Munger (his single most-quoted line)

> "I think I've been in the top 5% of my age cohort almost all my adult life in understanding the power of incentives, and yet I've always underestimated that power. Never a year passes but I get some surprise that pushes a little further my appreciation of incentive superpower."  — Munger

> "Never ask the barber if you need a haircut."  — Munger

> "Whose bread I eat, his song I sing."  — old proverb often invoked by Munger to explain analysts, advisors, and lobbyists

## 6. On the Market & Mr. Market

> "In the short run, the market is a voting machine. In the long run, it is a weighing machine."  — Benjamin Graham (constantly invoked by Buffett)

> "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient."  — Buffett

> "Mr. Market is there to serve you, not to guide you."  — Buffett (paraphrasing Graham)

> "The market is a no-called-strike game. You don't have to swing at everything — you can wait for your pitch."  — Buffett

## 7. On Bubbles & Euphoria

> "Nothing sedates rationality like large doses of effortless money."  — Buffett

> "The dumbest reason in the world to buy a stock is because it's going up."  — Buffett

> "Only when the tide goes out do you discover who's been swimming naked."  — Buffett

> "What the wise do in the beginning, fools do in the end."  — Buffett (often quoted in the late stages of bubbles)

> "When you combine ignorance and leverage, you get some pretty interesting results."  — Buffett

## 8. On Bear Markets & Fear

> "Be fearful when others are greedy and greedy when others are fearful."  — Buffett (the most quoted Buffett line of all)

> "The best chance to deploy capital is when things are going down."  — Buffett

> "Opportunities come infrequently. When it rains gold, put out the bucket, not the thimble."  — Buffett

## 9. On EBITDA & Accounting Tricks

> "Every time you see EBITDA, you should substitute the words 'bullshit earnings.'"  — Munger

> "Does management think the tooth fairy pays for capex?"  — Buffett (on excluding D&A)

> "If options aren't a form of compensation, what are they? If compensation isn't an expense, what is it? And if expenses shouldn't go into the calculation of earnings, where in the world should they go?"  — Buffett (on stock-based comp accounting)

> "Accounting is the language of business, and you have to be as comfortable with that as you are with your own native language to really evaluate businesses."  — Buffett

> "There is one excuse and only one — that we are dealing with a giant, malevolent force of nature — and even that doesn't really hold up."  — Munger (on creative accounting being passed off as routine)

## 10. On Capital Allocation

> "The CEO who misleads others in public may eventually mislead himself in private."  — Buffett (on promotional capital allocation justifications)

> "Most acquisitions are bad for the acquirer."  — Buffett

> "We don't get paid for activity, just for being right."  — Buffett

> "Diversification is protection against ignorance. It makes little sense for those who know what they're doing."  — Buffett (on excessive holdings — and applied to over-diversified M&A)

## 11. On Consensus & the Crowd

> "You can't buy what is popular and do well."  — Buffett

> "Mimicking the herd invites regression to the mean."  — Munger

> "The future is never clear; you pay a very high price in the stock market for a cheery consensus."  — Buffett

> "Most people get interested in stocks when everyone else is. The time to get interested is when no one else is. You can't buy what is popular and do well."  — Buffett

## 12. On Envy, Greed & FOMO

> "It is not greed that drives the world, it is envy."  — Munger

> "I think the best way to deal with the temptations of envy is to live a life where you are not in close contact with people who have what you want."  — Munger

> "Someone will always be getting richer faster than you. This is not a tragedy."  — paraphrased Munger (the sentiment appears throughout *Poor Charlie's Almanack*)

> "If you can't stomach 50% declines in your investment, you will get the mediocre returns you deserve."  — Munger

## 13. On Thinking, Learning & Changing Your Mind

> "I never allow myself to have an opinion on anything that I don't know the other side's argument better than they do."  — Munger

> "We all are learning, modifying, or destroying ideas all the time. Rapid destruction of your ideas when the time is right is one of the most valuable qualities you can acquire. You must force yourself to consider arguments on the other side."  — Munger

> "Spend each day trying to be a little wiser than you were when you woke up."  — Munger

> "I constantly see people rise in life who are not the smartest, sometimes not even the most diligent, but they are learning machines."  — Munger

> "If a thing is worth doing, it's worth doing right."  — Buffett

## 14. On Admitting Mistakes

> "Wide diversification is only required when investors do not understand what they are doing."  — Buffett

> "Knowing what you don't know is more useful than being brilliant."  — Munger

## 18. On Avoiding Stupidity (Munger's Specialty)

> "It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent."  — Munger

> "All I want to know is where I'm going to die so I'll never go there."  — Munger

> "If you're going to be an investor, you're going to make some investments where you don't have all the experience you need. But if you keep trying to get a little better over time, you'll start to make investments that are virtually certain to have a good outcome. The keys are discipline, hard work, and practice. It's like playing golf — you have to work on it."  — Munger

> "The first rule is don't lose. The second rule is don't forget the first rule."  — Buffett

## 22. On Twaddle & Vague Language

> "There's a lot of nonsense in modern academic finance."  — Munger

> "Twaddle has its place. But a man who can't think clearly enough to recognize twaddle is in big trouble."  — paraphrased Munger from *Poor Charlie's Almanack*

> "Tell me what you spend your money on, and I'll tell you what you value."  — Buffett (on cutting through stated values to revealed ones)

## 23. On Framing & Narratives

> "We all are learning, modifying, or destroying ideas all the time. Rapid destruction of your ideas when the time is right is one of the most valuable qualities you can acquire."  — Munger

> "If you would persuade, appeal to interest, not to reason."  — Munger (quoting Benjamin Franklin; he uses it constantly to explain why narratives work)

> "It's so easy to drift into evil... Just one small step at a time."  — Munger (on how compelling narratives slide reasonable people into bad decisions)

## 24. On Wall Street as an Institution

> "Wall Street is the only place that people ride to in a Rolls Royce to get advice from those who take the subway."  — Buffett

> "The stock market is filled with individuals who know the price of everything, but the value of nothing."  — Buffett (echoing Wilde)

> "Investment bankers, by and large, are paid to do deals."  — Buffett (an explanation, not a compliment)

> "When a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for bad economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact."  — Buffett

## How to Use This Quote Bank

Pick *one* quote per analysis. Two is forgivable; three becomes a wall hanging.

The right quote does three things:
1. Crystallizes the situation in language the user will remember
2. Lends authority — the user is hearing it from Buffett or Munger, not from you
3. Closes the analysis with a punch rather than a fade

When two quotes apply equally well, prefer the *shorter* one and the *more specific* one. "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome" beats a paragraph of explanation about why the analyst's report is biased.

If no quote in this bank fits the situation, the analysis isn't sharp enough yet, or the situation is novel enough that no Buffett/Munger frame really applies — in which case, name that explicitly rather than forcing a quote that almost-works.
